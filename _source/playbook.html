§ 01 · Thesis

Why PrivexBot is on social at all.

Most companies are on social because their competitors are. We're on social because the people who need us — privacy-burned engineers, compliance officers, regulated-industry product teams — don't know we exist yet. Our job is to find them.

The problem we exist to solve

Every AI tool in the market quietly assumes its users are willing to send their customers' data to three third parties. For consumer apps, that bargain is implicit and mostly accepted. For healthcare, fintech, legal, defense, government, education — the same bargain is illegal, unworkable, or career-ending. PrivexBot is the AI automation platform for teams that can't make that bargain.

We replace trust with proof. Every inference is cryptographically attested, so you don't have to take our word for anything. The PrivexBot pitch, in one sentence.

What our social presence is for

Three jobs, in priority order:

  1. Educate. Most of our future customers don't know that "private AI" is technically possible at the inference layer. Posts that explain TEEs, attestation, and the actual privacy gap in mainstream tools do the heaviest lifting.
  2. Convert. Once someone understands the gap, we want them to try PrivexBot inside fifteen minutes. Every post path leads to privexbot.com with the friction stripped.
  3. Recruit. Engineers who care about this stuff are rare. The people who already do this work for free in OSS communities are who we want to hire. Build-in-public posts double as a recruiting funnel.

What our social presence is not for

  • Brand awareness for awareness's sake. We don't optimise for impressions or follower counts as ends.
  • Reacting to news cycles that don't intersect AI privacy. Stay in lane.
  • Customer support. Route to support@privexbot.com, always.
  • Internal celebrations. Birthdays, anniversaries, "team off-site!" content — these live on the careers page, not the brand timeline.
The thesis test

Before any post ships, ask: does this educate, convert, or recruit? If the honest answer is "none of the above," it doesn't go out. Restraint is brand-building.

§ 02 · Platform strategy

Home, broadcast, placeholder.

We don't post everything everywhere. Each platform plays a defined role. Mis-cast a platform and you waste cycles on content that won't perform there.

The three roles

Home platforms are where conversation happens. We invest most of our energy here: native posts, replies, threads, long-form. Broadcast platforms are publishing channels — we make a polished thing and put it up, but we don't expect a comment section. Placeholder platforms exist so we're not absent when someone searches; we maintain a credible profile and re-share the best of what's already published elsewhere.

Cross-posting rules

  • Never cross-post raw. A LinkedIn post is not a tweet. A tweet is not an Instagram caption. Re-write the hook and trim or expand for the platform.
  • Repurpose vertically. One YouTube tutorial → three TikTok cuts → one IG carousel → one LinkedIn explainer. Same idea, four shapes.
  • Threads are platform-native. A 5-tweet thread on X becomes a single carousel on IG; never paste tweets into Instagram.
  • Hashtags go where hashtags work. Heavy on IG/TikTok. Light on LinkedIn (1–3, max). Almost none on X.
Channels we are intentionally not on

Threads, Mastodon, Bluesky. The audiences we care about aren't there yet at meaningful scale. Revisit quarterly. Discord: we run a community server but that's a product surface, not a marketing channel — different playbook, different team.

§ 03 · Voice & tone

Plainspoken. Specific. Confident. Calm.

Our nearest neighbours in voice are Linear's release notes, Supabase's docs, and Vercel's product pages. We're a developer tool that's growing up into an enterprise platform. The voice has to age well into both rooms.

The four attributes

Plainspoken. We use the simplest word that does the job. "Encrypt," not "leverage cryptographic primitives." "Read your prompts," not "process your inputs."

Specific. "Build a chatbot in fifteen minutes" beats "powerful AI for everyone." Numbers, named platforms, concrete tasks. Vague promises read as marketing; specifics read as truth.

Confident. No hedging, no "we think maybe," no "could possibly help." We state. If we can't state it, we don't post it.

Calm. Privacy is an anxious subject. The voice that earns trust here is the one that doesn't sound alarmed. We describe threats matter-of-factly and solutions equally so. No bold capitalisation. No exclamation points outside of jokes.

Vocabulary

Prefer
  • Encrypted at rest, encrypted in use — accurate, specific
  • Trusted Execution Environment (TEE) — name the tech
  • Attestation — the proof we offer
  • Chatflow — our visual builder noun
  • Agent — when referring to a deployed bot
  • Workspace — the user's home in the app
  • Build, deploy, verify — three verbs we own
Avoid
  • "Military-grade" — meaningless, makes us sound like a VPN ad
  • "AI-powered" — true of everything; says nothing
  • "Revolutionary," "game-changing," "next-gen" — empty
  • "Solutions" — we make a product, not solutions
  • "Leverage," "utilise," "operationalise" — say "use"
  • "Best-in-class" — let the work prove that
  • "Powered by AI" — what isn't, in 2026

Sentence case, always

Titles, headlines, CTAs, button labels — all sentence case. Read the changelog, not Read The Changelog. Exception: proper nouns and the brand name itself (PRIVEXBOT in the wordmark; PrivexBot in running text).

Em-dashes, semicolons, oxford commas

Em-dash (—) is welcome — it's a workhorse of our rhythm. Oxford comma always. Semicolons used sparingly, when two clauses are genuinely independent but related. Don't fear the period; short sentences land harder.

§ 04 · Visual system

Four lanes. One mark. Lots of whitespace.

Visual variety on social is good; visual chaos is not. We solve this by committing to four visual lanes. Every post lives in exactly one. Cross-lane combinations are how decks start looking like Canva freebies.

The four lanes

Mono (default)

White surface, ink-950 typography, signal-500 accents. The everyday lane. Use for tutorials, changelogs, build-in-public, comparisons. ~50% of feed posts live here.

Dark

Ink-950 surface, white type, signal indigo radial glows, sage attest highlights. The "infrastructure" lane — feels like a devtool homepage. Use for privacy/TEE explainers, launches, hot takes, anything that needs gravitas. ~30% of posts.

Light

Signal-50 or ink-25 surface, ink typography, signal-500 dominant. Friendlier SaaS feel. Use for stats, build-in-public, customer outcomes, CTAs. ~15% of posts.

Editorial

White surface with a dot grid texture, big display type, signal indigo accents, optional pull-quote treatments. Use for founder essays, manifestos, customer quotes, industry takes. ~5% of posts — rare on purpose.

Rule of one

One lane per post. One accent color per post. One display headline per post. If a layout feels weak, the fix is bigger type or more whitespace — not more decoration.

The logo

One mark, five colorways. The shape is fixed and never modified. Variants are colour-only:

  • Indigo on white — the default lockup
  • Black on white — when colour fidelity isn't guaranteed (print, partner decks)
  • White on dark — dark lane, video thumbnails, dark-mode profiles
  • Indigo light on signal-900 — high-contrast hero moments
  • Sage — reserved for security-themed assets only; never decorative

Clearspace

Minimum clearspace around the mark = the height of the "P" cap. Never crop into the mark. Never rotate, skew, stretch, or layer effects on it. Never put it inside another shape that competes for attention.

Wordmark

Wordmark is Inter Tight 800 in PRIVEXBOT capitals with 0.08em tracking. Always paired with the mark; never set on its own at large sizes. For body running text use sentence-case PrivexBot.

Typography

FamilyUseSizes
Inter TightDisplay headlines, hero type, wordmark48–144px, weight 700
InterBody, UI, captions, labels14–22px, weight 400/500/600
JetBrains MonoCode, API endpoints, eyebrows, metadata, technical tags11–16px, weight 400/500/600

Iconography

Use the Lucide icon set (1.5px stroke, currentColor). Never mix icon sets in one post. Never use a multi-colour icon. Never use emoji as an icon substitute — emoji is allowed sparingly as content (jokes, IG bio bullets, casual replies) but never as an interface element on a post template.

Photography & imagery

We don't use stock photography. Ever. Posts that need a visual element use one of three approaches:

  1. Type-led layouts — let the headline do the work (most posts)
  2. Geometric / abstract compositions — angled shapes, dot grids, radial gradients in brand colours
  3. Real product screenshots — when we can show actual chatflow UI, attestation receipts, or the visual builder

For customer/use-case posts that benefit from a face: a real photo, monochrome with a signal-500 duotone treatment. Never generic "diverse team smiling at laptop" stock.

✓ The screenshot test

Look at the post next to ten unrelated posts in the platform's native feed. If you can identify it as ours without seeing the avatar, the visual system is doing its job. If it could be Notion, Linear, Vercel, or a generic SaaS — pull it and start over.

Forbidden combinations

Approved
  • Ink-950 surface + signal-300/400 accents (Dark lane)
  • Ink-0 surface + signal-500 accents (Mono lane)
  • Signal-50 surface + signal-500/700 accents (Light lane)
  • Signal-500 surface + ink-0 type (CTA / hero buttons)
  • Any surface + attest-500 (security-themed only)
Never ship
  • Signal + attest as equal accents in one post (sage is reserved, not co-equal)
  • Gradients across more than two brand colours
  • Warn/danger as decoration (red is for crisis only)
  • Pure black (#000) — always use ink-950 for the slight warmth
  • White type on coloured backgrounds without a contrast check; always WCAG AA
  • Off-system colours introduced for "fun" — there is no off-system fun

Accessibility

Body text on any surface must clear WCAG AA contrast (4.5:1). Display type ≥ 24px must clear 3:1. ink-300 on ink-25 fails — never use it for body. signal-300 on ink-950 passes — that's the standard dark-lane accent pair. When in doubt, run the post through a contrast checker before publishing.

Six pillars. Every post slots into one.

If you can't identify the pillar in five seconds, the post needs sharpening. The pillars also drive cadence — each one gets a target percentage of monthly output.

Pillar deep dives

Privacy & TEE explainers

The flagship pillar. Posts explain what a Trusted Execution Environment is, what attestation is, what the actual privacy gap looks like in mainstream tools, what threat models PrivexBot defends against. Always concrete — never abstract "privacy matters!" content.

Sample hooks:

  • "Three companies read your prompt before the model does. Here's the receipt that proves we don't."
  • "What 'private' actually means at the inference layer."
  • "You can't promise privacy. You can prove it. Here's how."

Product feature drops

New nodes in the chatflow builder. New deploy targets. New integrations. Pricing changes. Roadmap unlocks. Keep it factual; let the screenshot or short demo do the persuading.

Sample hooks:

  • "WhatsApp deploys. Now generally available."
  • "Visual chatflow builder v2. 14 new nodes. Inline run inspector."
  • "Self-hostable Llama 3 70B. Same workspace. Same encryption."

Tutorials & how-tos

Step-by-step builds. The format that converts best — viewer sees they could ship the same thing in 15 minutes. Always end on a deployable artefact.

Sample hooks:

  • "Build a Discord support bot in 14 minutes."
  • "Add a 200-page PDF to your chatbot's brain."
  • "Connect your knowledge base to Slack — no API keys involved."

Use cases & customers

Real stories from real teams. Healthcare, legal, fintech, government, education — regulated industries are our home turf. Names where permitted; pseudonyms ("a 200-bed hospital in the Midwest") where not. Always include a quote and a metric.

Industry POV & hot takes

Positions on AI privacy, vendor opacity, the difference between "compliant" and "secure." Allowed to be sharp — never punching down at named competitors, always punching at industry practices.

Build-in-public

Engineering postmortems, traffic milestones, hiring stories, design process. Build credibility with the engineering audience that ends up either using or building PrivexBot.

§ 09 · Vertical formats

Stories, Reels, Shorts, TikTok.

Vertical surfaces have their own rules. Hook in 1.5 seconds. Single idea per video. On-screen text always — most viewers are scrolling without audio.

Vertical hook structure

Every vertical video follows the same five-beat structure:

  1. Hook (0–1.5s) — the question, claim, or visual pattern interrupt that makes someone stop scrolling
  2. Context (1.5–5s) — what we're about to show or claim
  3. Body (5–25s) — the actual content: demo, explanation, stat, takeaway
  4. Payoff (last 3–5s) — the insight, the answer, the "so what"
  5. CTA (final 2s, or in caption) — what to do next; for short formats often just a logo flash

On-screen text rules

  • Inter Tight 700, 48–80pt for hooks; 32–48pt for body. Never smaller than 28pt — TikTok compresses tiny text into mush.
  • White on ink-950 backdrop for maximum legibility. If overlaying live footage, drop a 70% opacity ink-950 plate behind the text.
  • One sentence at a time. Each thought gets its own card. No paragraphs on a screen.
  • Caption everything. Use auto-captions then proofread. Spell PrivexBot correctly every time (auto-caption defaults to "Privacy Bot").
  • No watermarks from external editors. CapCut watermarks specifically — strip them. Sponsored watermarks (TikTok's built-in) are fine.

Story / reel cover stickers

Five highlight cover designs live in the kit — Privacy, Tutorials, Customers, Changelog, Team. Use those exact covers for Instagram highlights. Don't create new ones; if a sixth category is needed, add it to the kit first.

Audio

  • Original audio preferred. Voiceovers, ambient room tone, keystroke clicks. It performs better in algorithm signals than trending music.
  • Trending audio is allowed for memes and lighthearted posts only. Never use trending audio under a privacy/TEE explainer — tonally wrong.
  • Music ducked under voiceover. If music plays during talk, drop it to -18db.

Some hashtags do more harm than good — they signal "marketing account" or attract bot engagement. Never use:

A note for compliance teams evaluating AI vendors:

When you send a message to a typical AI chatbot, three companies usually get to read it before the model generates a response. The AI vendor logs it for "service improvement." The model host logs it in server analytics. The network provider may capture it in transit.

None of these companies are doing anything wrong. They're just doing what their TOS allows. The problem is that "what TOS allows" and "what compliance teams in regulated industries can accept" are two different things.

PrivexBot runs every inference inside a hardware-attested Trusted Execution Environment. We can't read your prompts. Neither can the model host. Neither can the network. And we give you a cryptographic receipt for every inference, so your compliance team can verify it independently.

If your vendor's privacy policy starts with "we may use your data to improve our service," it's worth a conversation.

Posting times

Default to the audience timezone for each platform, not yours. The full spreadsheet of best slots lives in the team drive; the heuristic:

  • X: 09:00 + 16:00 ET, weekdays. Threads at 10:00 ET hold up best.
  • LinkedIn: 08:00–10:00 ET, Mon/Wed/Fri.
  • Instagram: 11:00 + 19:00 ET. Reels do well at 21:00 ET.
  • YouTube: Drop Tuesday 14:00 UTC = breakfast US east coast, mid-day Europe.
  • TikTok: 06:00 + 22:00 viewer-local (the platform splits across regions; scheduling tools handle this).

Sprint vs. baseline

Two modes:

  • Baseline (~46 weeks/year) — cadence above. Sustainable forever.
  • Sprint (~6 weeks/year, scheduled in advance) — 2× the post count, daily on every platform. Reserved for launch weeks, major customer announcements, conferences.

Don't sprint without a sprint-justifying reason. "It's been quiet" is not a reason; quiet is fine.

Reply & engagement targets

  • X mentions: respond within 4 hours (business hours), 24h max.
  • LinkedIn comments: respond within 24 hours. Like every comment within 12.
  • YouTube comments: within 48 hours; pin the best of each video.
  • DMs: within 24h with a route to the right place (support, sales, docs).
§ 12 · Guardrails

The never-list.

A short list of things we don't do, ever. These exist to make decisions fast at 11pm before a launch.

Always
  • Verify privacy claims with engineering before posting (anything technical)
  • Get customer permission in writing before naming them
  • Sign founder/exec quotes with the real human
  • Run a contrast/accessibility check on every designed post
  • Spell PrivexBot correctly (auto-caption tools default to "Privacy Bot")
  • Time-stamp data ("as of April 2026") on any stat we cite
  • Disclose paid partnerships visibly (FTC + EU rules apply)
  • Archive every post in the social drive within 7 days
Never
  • Screenshot customer prompts, even anonymised, even for explanatory purposes
  • Use stock photography
  • Make security claims we can't prove with attestation logs
  • Punch down at named competitors — punch at industry practices
  • Engage in political controversies that don't intersect AI privacy
  • Use AI-generated faces or voice for spokespeople; no AI "team" photos
  • Post during a major news tragedy without a 24h cooling period
  • Auto-DM new followers with a sales pitch
  • Buy followers, likes, comments, or engagement of any kind
  • Run "tag 3 friends to win" engagement-bait giveaways
  • Post about a customer outage before it's resolved + acknowledged on status page

Sensitive-topic protocol

When a news event touches our space — a major AI privacy breach, a regulatory ruling, an industry incident — follow this:

  1. Wait 24 hours. The first day is for journalists and victims, not vendors trying to capitalise.
  2. Check internally. Does this materially affect a customer? If yes, customer comms first.
  3. If we comment, comment substantively. Either we have something useful to say (an explainer, a position, a relevant feature) or we stay quiet. No "thoughts and prayers" + product link.
  4. Sign with a human if it's serious. Founder voice, not brand voice.

Crisis response (if we break or screw up)

  1. Status page first, social second. Always.
  2. Acknowledge fast. "We're aware. Investigating. Update in 30 min." Don't speculate.
  3. Post the resolution and the root cause publicly within 72 hours.
  4. Don't delete the original incident post. Add an update; let the history stand.
  5. Postmortem on the engineering blog within 2 weeks for anything that affected customer privacy or availability.

Legal & compliance reminders

  • HIPAA/PHI: never post anything that could be construed as patient data. Even a fake example from a healthcare customer needs legal review.
  • GDPR: testimonials with EU customers require explicit written consent for the post and the platforms it will appear on.
  • SOC2/ISO: we hold audit trails; treat customer logos and quotes as covered by our standard NDA — get the marketing rider signed.
  • Financial claims: any revenue, ARR, or customer-count number must be approved by the CFO. "Trusted by hundreds of teams" is fine; "$3.2M ARR" needs sign-off.

Approval flow

  1. Drafted in Notion (social → drafts board). Caption + visual + target platforms + scheduled date.
  2. Auto-routed for review based on pillar — privacy → eng, customer story → legal, etc.
  3. Approved drafts move to "Queued" column with publish date.
  4. Buffer / Hypefury picks up from "Queued" and ships on schedule.
  5. Published posts auto-log to the analytics dashboard with their pillar tag.

Review cycles

  • Weekly: social lead reviews the past week's post performance; flags any post under the threshold for retro.
  • Monthly: brand lead + social lead review pillar mix vs target. Adjust calendar if drift.
  • Quarterly: full team review of the playbook itself. What rules helped? What rules got in the way? What changed in the world that breaks an assumption here?
  • Annually: founders re-read the thesis section. If the company strategy moved, the thesis moves first; everything else follows.
